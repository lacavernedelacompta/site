Master your amortisation schedule in Belgium

A mortgage or professional loan is a long-term commitment. Understanding your amortisation schedule helps you manage your budget and negotiate the best terms with Belgian banks.

  • Monthly payments: each payment includes an interest portion and a capital repayment portion
  • Loan-to-value ratio: below 80% generally gives you better interest rates
  • Fixed vs variable rate: fixed offers security, variable may be more advantageous short-term
Tip: Simulate different durations (15, 20, 25 years) to find the optimal balance between monthly payment and total credit cost.

❓ Frequently asked questions about Belgian mortgage loans

What is the difference between constant annuity and constant capital repayment ?▼

With constant annuity, you pay the same monthly instalment throughout the loan, but the interest portion decreases progressively. With constant capital, the capital repayment stays fixed, but monthly payments decrease over time.

How is a Belgian loan amortisation table calculated ?▼

The amortisation table breaks down each monthly payment into capital repaid and interest paid. For a €200,000 loan at 3.5% over 25 years, the monthly payment is approximately €1,001.

What is the average interest rate for a Belgian mortgage in 2026 ?▼

In 2026, fixed rates for a 20-year mortgage generally range between 3% and 4.5% depending on the bank and borrower profile. Always compare the APR for a fair comparison.

Can you make early repayment on a Belgian mortgage ?▼

Yes, early repayment is legally possible. For fixed-rate loans, a reinvestment fee may apply, capped at 3 months' interest on the repaid amount.
